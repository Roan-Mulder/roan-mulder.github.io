Platformer Level Design Toolkit (2025-2026)

Level Designer, Solo Project

Cover image: mario-cover.jpg

Summary

A modular toolkit for Unreal Engine 5 that rebuilds the obstacles of Super Mario Sunshine's secret levels (the ones without FLUDD) as Actor Components and Blueprints you can drop into your own level. It comes with a 26-page How-To document and a demo level that shows every element in use.

The goal

The secret levels in Super Mario Sunshine are built from a small set of obstacles that keep coming back. I wanted to break them down into reusable parts and turn them into a toolkit that anyone can use to build their own platforming levels.

The toolkit is made for three groups:

  • Beginners in Unreal Engine 5 who want to try level design without a lot of Blueprint work.
  • Fans of 3D platformers like Super Mario Sunshine or A Hat in Time who want to recreate those levels.
  • Advanced Unreal Engine 5 users who want to expand an existing platformer toolkit.

How I designed it

My first proposal was a Banjo-Kazooie character controller. I changed the concept in week 2 and took the Mario Sunshine proposal through three drafts and a round of feedback.

I sorted the obstacles I found into must have, should have, could have and won't have, so the scope stayed realistic. After feedback that the proposal was too simple, I looked at other games for more behaviors, including balloons, button sequences and trampolines from A Hat in Time, a shockwave hazard from Penny's Big Breakaway and the switch block from Super Mario 64.

Before building each element, I painted over reference screenshots to show the movement direction, rotation axes and states of the behavior.

What is in the toolkit

  • Simple Movement
  • Simple Rotation
  • Disappearing Platform
  • Bouncing Platform
  • Spline Moving Platform
  • Platforming Void Actor
  • End Collectable
  • Regular Collectable

The How-To document has a chapter for each element, a complete level setup guide, a section on adding new content to the toolkit, and a troubleshooting section.

Playtesting and iteration

My lecturer played the demo level and gave feedback on several things. I made two rounds of changes.

Before the playtest

Image: mario-iteration-0.png

Round 1: making the demo a showcase

Image: mario-iteration-1.png

The resting points had no information, so I added short text explaining what the next section teaches. I also added collectables as red coins that unlock the end collectable, and an extra resting spot, which meant moving sections 4 and 5 further right.

Round 2: clearer to read, kinder to play

Image: mario-iteration-2.png

The all-white blocks made it hard to judge space, so I made six simple color materials and kept the resting points white to mark them as safe. I made a cube with a different color on each side, so you can see which way a platform is rotating. Sections 3, 4 and 5 were too punishing, so I added floors under the hardest parts, because the level is a showcase and should not punish the player.
